The puzzle I want to solve is why emotion becomes an emotional disorder — the mechanism by which adaptive emotion develops into affective psychopathology. What makes this puzzle compelling is that it has two faces at once: a set of symptom patterns shared across a clinical population, and individual differences that this shared pattern alone cannot explain.

I believe emotion largely stems from, and can be explained through, the human body — the brain and autonomic nervous system respond to stimuli, while cognition receives and interprets those responses. My work aims to disentangle the shared and idiosyncratic mechanisms of affective disorders through a multimodal approach that integrates neuroimaging, peripheral physiology, and self-report.
