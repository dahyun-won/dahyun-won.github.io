Intrinsic connectivity signatures of anxiety sensitivity via connectome-based predictive modeling

Manuscript submitted · Human Brain Mapping Poster presented · Society of Biological Psychiatry

Overview

We identified whole-brain intrinsic connectivity patterns predictive of individual differences in anxiety sensitivity, a fear of anxiety-related sensations based on beliefs that they may have harmful physical, cognitive, or social consequences, applying connectome-based predictive modeling (CPM) to resting-state fMRI data from a non-clinical sample (N = 86). Higher anxiety sensitivity was primarily predicted by reduced intrinsic connectivity, particularly in insula-temporal, insula-frontal, and temporal-parietal connections. Anxiety sensitivity is reflected in distributed intrinsic connectivity rather than alterations in a single region.

  • Established and led resting-state fMRI data preprocessing, conducted the analysis (connectome-based predictive modeling), and drafted the manuscript.

Universality and cultural specificity in misophonia: A cross-national study

Manuscript · In prep Flash talk and poster to be presented · Society for Research in Psychopathology Ongoing data collection (MRI session)

Overview

In an international research collaboration (Yonsei University, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, and University of São Paulo), we identified cross-national similarities and differences in sound aversion profiles among people with misophonia (N = 449). These national differences appear to reflect sociocultural norms and urbanicity-driven sound exposure. Furthermore, the misophonia effect was moderated by nation, with greater amplification of aversion for sounds that each nation found more aversive.

Functional connectivity patterns underlying children’s epistemic curiosity and question efficiency

Poster presented and awarded · Korean Society for Developmental Psychology Poster presented · International Society for the Study of Behavioural Development Ongoing data collection

Overview

Using region-of-interest (ROI)-to-ROI functional connectivity analysis (N = 34), we identified differential intrinsic connectivity patterns between children with interest-type curiosity and those with deprivation-type curiosity. These two curiosity subtypes recruit opposing neural modes: interest-type curiosity suppresses connectivity between the anterior cingulate cortex, lateral prefrontal cortex, and ventral striatum, whereas deprivation-type curiosity enhances these connections. Also, both deprivation-type curiosity and question efficiency were linked to stronger connectivity between the ventral tegmental area and ventral striatum, suggesting a shared dopaminergic basis.

Duties

  • Implemented the resting-state fMRI data preprocessing pipeline and conducted analysis (ROI-to-ROI functional connectivity).
