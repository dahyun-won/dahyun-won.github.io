Intrinsic connectivity signatures of anxiety sensitivity via connectome-based predictive modeling

Submitted · Human Brain Mapping Leading role

Anxiety sensitivity (AS) — the fear of anxiety-related sensations — is a transdiagnostic vulnerability factor whose intrinsic neural architecture is poorly understood. Using connectome-based predictive modeling (CPM) on resting-state fMRI from a non-clinical sample (N = 86), I identified whole-brain intrinsic connectivity patterns that significantly predict individual differences in AS (prediction R2 = .07, pperm < .005), characterized primarily by reduced insula–temporal, insula–frontal, and temporo-parietal connectivity. An exploratory follow-up applied the trained model to an independent clinical sample to test whether it generalized to panic symptom severity.

Key point — anxiety sensitivity is reflected in distributed intrinsic connectivity involving the insula and broader cortical systems, rather than in a single region.

Universality and cultural specificity in misophonia: a cross-national study

In prep Leading role

An international collaboration (Yonsei University, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, University of São Paulo) asking whether the sound aversion profiles of people with misophonia are universal or culturally specific. Across 449 participants in Korea, the US, and Brazil rating the unpleasantness of 50 sounds, we found both cross-national similarities and differences in aversion profiles, and the gap in profiles between misophonia and control groups was itself moderated by nation. I lead the fMRI arm of this project — revising the interoceptive-attention and trigger-sound fMRI task designs, drafting and managing IRB protocols, and programming the cross-nationally distributed behavioral task — with an MRI data collection wave upcoming.

Key point — misophonia is not a culturally invariant phenomenon: the US and Brazil showed greater aversion to eating sounds relative to Korea, while Korea and Brazil showed greater aversion to urban and indoor sounds relative to the US.

Multimodal physiology infrastructure for interoception research

Methods & infrastructure

Within the lab's larger interoception project, I built and validated the data-collection pipeline: a heartbeat-tapping task for interoceptive modulation integrated with BIOPAC MP150 hardware, resting-state HRV acquisition (ECG, PPG), and an eye-tracker synchronized with fMRI tasks for real-time pupillometry — including the exclusion criteria used to keep the resulting datasets analysis-ready.

Curiosity and functional connectivity across development

Cross-lab collaboration

In collaboration with the Human Development Lab, I implemented the resting-state fMRI preprocessing pipeline and ran ROI-to-ROI functional connectivity analyses examining how different types of curiosity relate to resting-state connectivity across childhood and adolescence.
